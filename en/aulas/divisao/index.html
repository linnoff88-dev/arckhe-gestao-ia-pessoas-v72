Lesson 2 · 18 min

What stays with people

Hora came on board and no one told Juliana what was changing for her. She found out on her own: the easy questions vanished from her queue, the hard ones stayed. Splitting the work is a management decision, made beforehand, task by task, with the reason written down. In this lesson you decide what stays with the person, what goes to the agent, and what both do together.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1List the tasks the way the person really works
  2. 2The four questions that decide the column
  3. 3Design the "both together": who prepares, who decides
  4. 4Decide where the freed-up time goes, without promising savings

Goal: decide, task by task, what stays with the person, what goes to the agent, and what both do together, with the reason written down.

02 · From list to matrix

Four steps. The matrix for Patrícia's team is practice; the one for your team is the one that counts.

1. List how work is done

The job description says "customer service." The person's day has twenty different tasks, some that nobody knows they do. List them with the person, looking at one real week.

For each task, note who does it today and how often. This list, not the org chart, is what the matrix divides.

At Horizonte (fictional study company)Juliana listed 9 tasks; Patrícia didn't even know about three of them, such as retyping into the tracking spreadsheet the orders the order app already records.

2. Run each task through the four questions

Does it go away? Does it depend on relationships or judgment about people? Does someone have to answer for the decision? Is it repeated, rule-based, and cheap to undo? The first "yes" decides the column.

Write the reason in one sentence. Without a reason, the split becomes opinion, and opinion changes with every complaint.

At Horizonte"Customer complaining about a delay": relationship, stays with Juliana. "Delivery time to Americana": rule and volume, goes to Hora.

3. Design the "both"

The middle column is the most common and the worst designed. For each task in it, write what the agent hands over ready, who decides, and within how long.

If nobody is named on the decision, the task fell to the agent without anyone noticing. The authority limit for each action is in G3; here the question is whose work it is.

At HorizonteNew customer: Hora collects the data and the CNPJ; Juliana checks and registers. Discount from 5% to 10%: Hora prepares; Patrícia approves.

4. Decide where the time goes

Taking a task off someone frees up hours, and freed hours are not savings. They only become results if you decide what the person does next, and tell them.

Don't promise what hasn't been decided. "Your time goes to checking registrations and the queue with me" is a decision; "nobody will change roles" is a promise.

At HorizonteThe time Juliana spent on the catalog goes to checking new registrations and to the exception queue with Patrícia. The salespeople get more time for visits.

Time freed: Juliana moves to checking registrations and the exception queue with Patrícia (agreed on 10/22). Salespeople: more visits.

  1. A list of the job, not of the day. Without talking to the person, the invisible tasks are missing.
  2. Approving in column A. If the agent approves on its own, nobody answers for the decision.
  3. "Both" with no name. Without who decides and a deadline, the task fell to the agent and nobody saw it.
  4. Skipping "does it go away?" Splitting a useless task between person and agent is double waste.
  5. Promising instead of deciding. Say what the person will do next; don't guarantee what hasn't been decided.

04 · Practice

Split the work of Patrícia's team; then your own team's, together with them.

1 · Patrícia's team matrix

25 min

Use Hora's setup and the lab's internal survey to list the commercial team's work (Juliana, salespeople, Patrícia). Run each task through the four questions and fill in the matrix. Run the review prompt and fix whatever makes sense.

Done when: the matrix has at least 10 tasks, uses all four columns (S, P, 2 and A), every row has its reason, every "2" task has who decides and a deadline, no task of approving, signing or registering is in column A, and the destination of Juliana's time is written down.

2 · Your team's matrix

40 min

Pick one person on your team and list with them the tasks of one real week. Build the matrix. Show it to them and write down what they corrected.

Done when: the matrix has at least 8 tasks, each with a column and a reason, the person has read it, at least one change was made because of what they said, and what to do with the freed-up time was agreed with them, not promised.
